# Automotive Communication Protocols Interview Questions

| No. | Interview question | Detailed answer |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | Compare UART, I2C, SPI, CAN, and Ethernet. | UART is asynchronous point-to-point serial communication. I2C is addressed, shared, two-wire, and suited to board peripherals. SPI is synchronous, fast, full-duplex, and uses chip selects. CAN is robust, prioritized, message-based vehicle networking. Ethernet offers much higher bandwidth and switched networking but requires a larger protocol, timing, and security stack. |
| 2 | How would you debug corrupted UART output during board bring-up? | I verify voltage levels, ground, pin mux, baud rate, data bits, parity, stop bits, clock accuracy, and flow control. A logic analyzer distinguishes electrical or timing problems from software buffer corruption. I begin with a known repeating pattern and polling mode, then add interrupts, DMA, buffering, and higher data rates incrementally. |
| 3 | What commonly causes an I2C device not to respond? | Likely causes include wrong address, missing pull-ups, voltage mismatch, incorrect pin mux, held-low lines, timing configuration, power or reset state, and incorrect repeated-start behavior. I inspect SDA and SCL with a logic analyzer, check ACK bits and rise time, then use bus-recovery clock pulses or peripheral reset if a slave is stuck. |
| 4 | What SPI parameters must match between controller and peripheral? | Both sides must agree on clock polarity and phase, bit order, word size, maximum clock rate, chip-select polarity and timing, and transaction framing. Signal integrity and voltage also matter. A logic-analyzer decode with a known register transaction quickly reveals shifted bits, wrong sampling edges, incorrect byte order, or chip-select gaps. |
| 5 | How does CAN arbitration work? | CAN uses non-destructive bitwise arbitration. Dominant bits overwrite recessive bits while nodes monitor the bus, so the frame with the numerically lowest identifier continues without corruption. Identifiers therefore encode priority, not merely an address. Designers must allocate priorities and bus load so low-priority messages still meet latency requirements. |
| 6 | What do CAN error-active, error-passive, and bus-off mean? | Nodes maintain transmit and receive error counters. An error-active node can transmit active error flags. After sufficient errors it becomes error-passive and limits disruption. Excessive transmit errors lead to bus-off, where it stops participating. Diagnosis examines counters, error frames, bit timing, termination, wiring, and whether recovery follows system safety policy. |
| 7 | How would you diagnose missing CAN messages with CANoe? | I verify channel configuration, bitrate, database, filters, network state, and whether the transmitter is bus-off. Trace and statistics show frames, error events, cycle times, counters, and bus load. I compare expected send conditions and signal values, then correlate ECU logs and debugger state to determine whether generation, transmission, network, or reception failed. |
| 8 | What should be validated for automotive Ethernet integration? | I validate physical link and negotiation, VLANs, addressing, routing, multicast, SOME/IP or other application protocols, service discovery, bandwidth, latency, packet loss, time synchronization, diagnostics, and security controls. Packet captures are correlated with ECU logs. Load, startup ordering, link interruption, malformed traffic, and recovery behavior are important integration scenarios. |
| 9 | When would you use CAN rather than automotive Ethernet? | CAN remains appropriate for deterministic, low-bandwidth control and sensor traffic where robustness, cost, mature diagnostics, and simple nodes matter. Ethernet suits cameras, high-volume data, software-oriented services, and backbone communication. Many vehicles use both, connected through gateways. The decision follows bandwidth, latency, topology, safety, security, and lifecycle requirements. |
| 10 | How do protocol tests become part of CI? | Software simulations and virtual buses run fast checks on every change, while scheduled bench jobs use CAN interfaces, Ethernet switches, logic analyzers, or HIL equipment for physical behavior. Test definitions, databases, captures, firmware identity, and verdicts are versioned. Resource locking, deterministic reset, health checks, and artifact retention make failures reproducible. |
