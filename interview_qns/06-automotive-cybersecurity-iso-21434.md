# Automotive Cybersecurity and ISO/SAE 21434 Interview Questions

| No. | Interview question | Detailed answer |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | What does ISO/SAE 21434 cover? | ISO/SAE 21434 defines cybersecurity engineering across the road-vehicle lifecycle, including governance, project management, concept, product development, production, operations, maintenance, and decommissioning. It is risk-based and work-product oriented. It does not prescribe one technical architecture; organizations must demonstrate appropriate activities, traceability, competence, controls, and evidence for the product context. |
| 2 | Explain the main steps of a TARA. | A Threat Analysis and Risk Assessment identifies assets and damage scenarios, derives threat scenarios and attack paths, assesses impact and attack feasibility, determines risk, and selects treatment. Cybersecurity goals and requirements follow from unacceptable risks. Assumptions, interfaces, operational environment, rationale, and traceability are essential because risk results change when system context changes. |
| 3 | How are assets, damage scenarios, and threat scenarios different? | An asset is something whose cybersecurity property has value, such as firmware authenticity or camera data integrity. A damage scenario describes potential adverse consequences to stakeholders if that property is compromised. A threat scenario describes how an attacker could cause the compromise. Keeping them distinct prevents jumping directly from a technical weakness to an unsupported risk rating. |
| 4 | How do you assess attack feasibility? | ISO/SAE 21434 allows suitable attack-feasibility methods using factors such as elapsed time, expertise, knowledge, opportunity or access, and equipment. Ratings require evidence and a defined method. I consider the complete attack path and realistic vehicle context, document assumptions, and revisit the result when architecture, interfaces, tooling, or public knowledge changes. |
| 5 | What is the relationship between cybersecurity goals and requirements? | Cybersecurity goals are high-level, solution-independent outcomes derived from risk treatment, such as preventing unauthorized firmware execution. They are refined into functional and technical requirements allocated to system, hardware, software, production, and operational controls. Bidirectional traceability connects risk, goal, requirements, design, implementation, verification, and residual-risk acceptance. |
| 6 | How would you provide cybersecurity evidence from a CI/CD pipeline? | The pipeline can preserve reviewed source identity, approved dependencies, SAST and SCA results, SBOM, test reports, signing records, checksums, access logs, approvals, and artifact provenance. Evidence must be tied to the exact candidate and retained under configuration management. Automation increases consistency, but accountable owners still review deviations and judge whether requirements are satisfied. |
| 7 | What is a Cybersecurity Case? | A Cybersecurity Case is a structured argument, supported by evidence, that cybersecurity goals for an item are adequately achieved. It links claims to requirements, analyses, verification, process compliance, and accepted residual risk. It is not simply a folder of reports; the reasoning must show why the evidence is sufficient and expose assumptions or unresolved gaps. |
| 8 | How does UNECE R155 relate to ISO/SAE 21434? | UNECE R155 requires manufacturers to operate a Cybersecurity Management System and demonstrate vehicle-type cybersecurity across development, production, and post-production. ISO/SAE 21434 provides an engineering framework that can support this evidence, but compliance with the standard does not automatically equal regulatory approval. Organizational processes and vehicle-specific implementation both matter. |
| 9 | How does UNECE R156 differ from R155? | R155 focuses on cybersecurity management and vehicle cybersecurity risks. R156 focuses on the Software Update Management System, including controlled update processes, integrity, compatibility, records, and safe execution. They overlap because software updates are a cybersecurity-sensitive lifecycle capability, but an organization must address the distinct management-system and vehicle-type obligations of each regulation. |
| 10 | How should cybersecurity be maintained after production? | Teams need vulnerability monitoring, incident response, fleet and version visibility, supplier coordination, risk reassessment, secure update capability, and defined support periods. New CVEs or attack techniques are evaluated against actual product configurations and exposure. Decisions, mitigations, customer or authority communication, deployed updates, and residual risks remain traceable throughout operations and decommissioning. |
