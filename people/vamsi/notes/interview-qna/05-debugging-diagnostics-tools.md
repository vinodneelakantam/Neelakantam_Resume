# Debugging & Diagnostics Tooling Interview Questions

| No. | Interview question | Detailed answer |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | How do you use Lauterbach/Trace32 during embedded debugging? | I use it to halt the target at a specific instruction or breakpoint, inspect memory/register state, and step through boot or fault-handling code that isn't visible from bus traces alone. It's my go-to when a fault-injection result looks correct on the bus but I need to confirm the internal state that produced it. |
| 2 | What's your approach to JTAG-based debugging on ECUs? | I use JTAG for low-level access when the target hasn't reached a state where higher-level diagnostics (UDS) are available yet, such as during early boot or bootloader debugging. It's slower to set up than bus-level tools, so I reserve it for cases where UART traces and DTCs don't explain the failure. |
| 3 | How do you perform root cause analysis (RCA) for an integration defect? | I reproduce the failure on demand (ideally via FIU or a scripted trigger), narrow the cause using bus traces and debugger state, and confirm the hypothesis by making a controlled change that either fixes or shifts the failure predictably. RCA isn't complete until I can explain why the defect didn't surface earlier in the pipeline, since that often reveals a coverage gap too. |
| 4 | How do you use a PE Micro Multilink or ICAS1 debugger for gateway ECU debugging? | These give me register- and memory-level access to the gateway ECU during bring-up or a hard-to-reproduce fault, letting me correlate internal state transitions with what CANoe shows on the bus. I use them together: bus trace tells me what happened externally, the debugger tells me why. |
| 5 | How do you validate software downloads (SWDL) on an ECU? | I verify the flash sequence completes without security-access or checksum errors, confirm the ECU reports the expected software version afterward, and re-run a baseline smoke test to confirm the new software boots and communicates correctly before handing the ECU back for full test execution. |
| 6 | How do you use VW ODIS or similar OEM diagnostic tooling in your workflow? | I use ODIS for standardized flashing, diagnostic session control, and DTC verification against the OEM's expected diagnostic behavior, which complements the lower-level debugger/bus-trace view with the same diagnostic interface a workshop tool would use. |
| 7 | How do you decide whether a defect is caused by hardware, software, or the test bench itself? | I isolate variables one at a time: same test on a different rig rules out bench hardware, same ECU on a known-good rig rules out the unit under test, and reverting to a previous software baseline rules out a recent software change. Only after eliminating these do I treat it as a genuine new software defect. |
| 8 | How do you document and hand off a debugging investigation to another engineer or team? | I record the exact reproduction steps, the fault/trigger condition, the traces and debugger state that support my conclusion, and what I've already ruled out. This lets the next engineer continue from where I stopped instead of re-deriving the same evidence. |
